# What Information Should a Pet Sitter Collect From Pet Parents?

**Before the first visit, a pet sitter should have the pet's routine, health and medication details, emergency contacts, home access instructions, and the client's update preferences, all in one written place.** If you ever have to look something up in a hurry, such as the vet's phone number or which door the dog uses, you do not want to scroll through months of text messages. A one-page profile for each pet solves that.

This guide lists what to collect, how to organize it, and what to leave out.

## Why use a written intake sheet instead of relying on memory?

Memory may be enough for one client, but it gets unreliable with ten, especially during busy periods. A written sheet also helps both sides: the client can see you captured their instructions, and you have a reference for what you were asked to do.

You can collect the information by form, email, or in person at the meet-and-greet. What matters is that it ends up in one document per pet, and that the client has a chance to confirm it.

## What should the pet profile include?

Group the details so they are easy to scan.

**Basics**
- Name, species, breed or type, approximate age
- Microchip status, if the client wants to share it
- Photos you can use to recognize the pet (helpful for dogs who look alike)

**Daily routine**
- Food type, amount, and times
- Water and treat rules
- Walk length, route, and equipment
- Potty habits and where the pet goes
- Sleeping spots and bedtime routine

**Health and care**
- Medication names, doses, and timing, as the client provides them
- Allergies, past injuries, or things to watch for
- Vet clinic name, phone number, and address
- Whether the client has authorized you to seek care and any spending limit they set

**Behavior notes**
- Fears and triggers
- Known escape habits
- How the pet gets along with other animals and strangers
- Commands the pet knows

**Home and access**
- Key, lockbox, or door code arrangements
- Alarm instructions
- Where supplies, waste bags, and cleaning items are kept
- Parking and building rules, if relevant

**Contacts**
- The client's phone and email while they are away
- A backup person who can be reached
- Travel dates and time zone, if the client is traveling

**Communication preferences**
- How often the client wants updates
- How many photos
- Whether they want a message for routine visits or only when something changes

## How should you handle medication information?

Write it down exactly as the client gives it to you, and read it back to them. Do not adjust a dose or timing on your own. If the instructions are unclear, ask. If you notice a change in how the pet responds to a medication, tell the client and suggest contacting their vet.

## How do you keep the information accurate?

Pets change. A dog starts a new food, a cat develops a preference, a key code is updated. Build a small habit:

1. **Review the sheet before each stay.** Send the client a short message: "Anything changed since last time?"
2. **Date your updates.** Add a note when you change something, so you know what is current.
3. **Keep one version.** Multiple copies across your phone, email, and a notebook make errors more likely.

## What should you avoid collecting or storing?

Collect what the job requires, and not more.

- **Payment card numbers in your notes.** Use a payment method designed for that purpose.
- **Alarm codes in plain, unprotected text.** Use a password-protected notes app or a locked notebook, and ask yourself whether you need the code written down at all.
- **Personal details unrelated to the job.** Keep the sheet focused on the animal and the access.
- **Photos of the home's interior that you do not need.**

Privacy rules differ by location and business type. If you are unsure what applies to you, check with a local business resource or an attorney.

## How do you store it so you can find it quickly?

Name each file with the client and pet, such as "Rivera - Luna". Keep the profile on your phone, since you will need it at the door, and protect that phone with a passcode. If you share a business with a partner or assistant, agree on who can see what.
