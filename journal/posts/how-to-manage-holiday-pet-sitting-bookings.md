# How to Manage Holiday Pet Sitting Bookings Without Burning Out

**The way to handle a holiday rush is to decide your limits before requests arrive: how many visits you can do per day, which dates you will work, what you will charge, and when bookings close.** Holiday weeks can bring a flood of requests from clients and from people you have never met. If you answer them one at a time without a plan, it is easy to overbook and then deliver rushed visits.

Here is a step-by-step approach for independent sitters and dog walkers.

## Why do holiday weeks get overwhelming?

Clients may travel at the same time, so requests can arrive close together and ask for the same dates. Holiday visits can also be longer or more demanding, with multiple visits per day for a stretch. And you may want time with your own family.

A plan keeps you from making decisions while tired or pressured.

## How do you decide how many bookings you can take?

Work backward from your own capacity.

1. **Count the hours, not just the visits.** Include driving time, setup, notes, and breaks.
2. **Mark the days you are unavailable.** Block them in your calendar first, before accepting anything.
3. **Set a daily maximum.** Choose a number of visits you can do well, and a gap between them for travel.
4. **Leave a buffer.** Allow room for an emergency, a late return, or a sick pet that needs extra time.

If you are not sure how many visits you can handle, start lower. Turning down a booking is easier than breaking a promise.

## What should a holiday policy include?

Put it in writing and send it to existing clients before the season starts. Items many sitters include:

- **Holiday dates and rates.** If you charge a surcharge, state the dates and amounts clearly.
- **Booking deadline.** For example, "Requests for Thanksgiving week must be in by November 10."
- **Minimum notice for changes.** When a client can change dates without a fee.
- **Cancellation terms.** What happens if plans change close to the date.
- **Deposit or payment timing,** if you use them.
- **Meet-and-greet requirement for new clients.** Plan these earlier in the month, not the week of.

These items are only a starting point. The right terms depend on your business, your local laws, and your clients, so confirm what applies to you.

## How do you prioritize requests?

Some sitters choose an order and apply it consistently:

1. Existing clients with active, ongoing schedules
2. Past clients who have booked before
3. New clients who have completed a meet-and-greet
4. Everyone else, as space allows

Whatever order you pick, tell people. A short message such as "I open holiday booking to regular clients first, then to new clients from November 1" avoids hurt feelings.

## How do you say no, and manage a waitlist?

You can decline without apologizing at length.

> Thank you for thinking of me. I am fully booked for that week. If a spot opens, would you like me to add you to my waitlist? If you need a backup right away, I can share the name of a sitter I trust.

Keep a simple waitlist with name, dates, pet, and the date they asked. If a slot opens, contact people in the order they asked. If you recommend another sitter, only do so if you know their work.

## How do you keep updates consistent when you are busy?

Updates can slip when you are juggling many visits a day, and clients who are away may be in another time zone. Two habits keep them steady:

- **Agree on the format before the season starts.** For example, a couple of photos and a short note after each visit, and a quicker message if something changes.
- **Send each update before you head to the next visit,** so you are not sorting a whole day of photos at night.

If you stack many visits in a day, build in a few minutes between them for this.

## How do you protect your own time off?

Block your own holiday first, tell clients the dates you are unavailable, and stick to it. Rest helps you do good work. If you rely on a backup sitter or helper, confirm availability and communicate clearly with clients before the season. Check whether any insurance or local rules apply to using helpers.
