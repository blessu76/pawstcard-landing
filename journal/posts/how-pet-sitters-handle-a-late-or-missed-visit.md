# How Pet Sitters Handle a Late or Missed Visit

**If you know you are going to be late, call the client before they start wondering, with a new arrival time and one reassuring line. If a visit is missed entirely, the goal shifts to the pet: get the pet looked after, tell the client quickly and honestly, and make it right.** Clients tend to remember how you handled the problem longer than they remember the problem itself.

This is general guidance for independent pet sitters and dog walkers. Follow any agreement you have with the client and any rules that apply where you work.

## What should you do when you know you are running late?

Reach out before the client starts wondering, not after. Keep the message to four things: the delay, the new time, one reassuring detail, and a promise to keep them updated.

> Hi Sam, I am stuck behind an accident on the bridge and will be about 25 minutes late. I will text you when I leave the parking lot and again when I am in the yard. I will make sure Milo gets his walk.

Do not over-explain. A short, specific message calms a client down more than a long story does. If the delay threatens the visit itself, say so plainly: "I will not make 4 pm. I am looking at a backup and will call you within the hour."

## Who can cover the pet if you cannot make the visit?

A solo sitter has no teammate to call, so the answer needs to be arranged before the bad day, not during it. Practical options:

- **A neighbor, friend, or family member the client already knows,** who can step in for one visit: feed, potty, fifteen minutes of play.
- **The client's own network.** A coworker, a relative, a friend of the family. Some clients prefer this anyway.
- **Another sitter you have worked with,** if you have a small professional circle and both clients are okay with it.

Bring it up at the meet-and-greet, lightly: "If I ever could not make a visit, who would you rather I call in, a neighbor you know or someone from your side?" Write the answer down. If you cannot line up anyone, call the client right away and let them decide who should step in.

## What do you say when a visit is missed?

Send the message as soon as you know you missed it, and lead with the pet.

> I am so sorry, I am writing to tell you I will miss today's 4 pm visit. Here is what I am doing: I have asked your neighbor Dana to feed Luna and take her out, and I will be there at 6:30 to make up the potty break and play time. I will text you the moment I am in.

Three parts: what happened, what is happening for the pet right now, what you will do next. Avoid long explanations of why. Most clients care about exactly one thing: is the pet okay, and is someone handling it.

## How do you make a missed visit right?

Match the make-up to the size of the miss, and offer it yourself rather than waiting to be asked.

- **One late visit.** A short apology plus a promise to be early next time is usually enough.
- **One missed visit, covered well.** A make-up visit at your cost, or a credit toward the next visit.
- **A repeat problem.** A conversation, a refund for the affected days, and a look at your schedule. If the schedule is the cause, fix the schedule.

In the follow-up, state the decision plainly: "I have credited today's visit, and I am booking a make-up play session for Thursday at no charge." Clients forgive an honest miss with a plan. They do not forgive a silent one.

## How do you prevent late and missed visits?

Most late visits are scheduling problems, not character problems:

- **Leave buffers.** Do not book visits back to back across town. Leave 20 to 30 minutes between homes.
- **Start the day early.** If you leave for the first visit five minutes late, the whole day is five minutes late.
- **Know every second entrance, gate, and parking spot** before the first visit, not during a delay.
- **Build in slack** for weather, school-run traffic, and the commute itself.
- **Set a phone reminder 15 minutes before each visit** that says the client's name, not just the time.

A schedule with slack turns a missed visit into a rare event instead of a recurring one.
