# How to Respond to a Pet Sitting Client Complaint

**When a client is unhappy, the best response is to listen fully, check your own notes, reply calmly in writing, and agree on one clear next step.** Many pet sitters will get a complaint at some point. How you handle it often matters more to the relationship than the complaint itself.

This is general guidance for independent pet sitters and dog walkers, not legal or business advice. If a complaint involves an injury, a dispute over money, or a legal threat, consider getting professional advice before you reply.

## Why do complaints feel so personal?

Pet care is trusting someone with a member of the family and with the keys to their home. A complaint can feel like an attack on your character, especially if you work alone and care about your clients. It helps to remember that the client is often worried about their pet, not trying to hurt you. Give yourself a little time before you answer so that your first reply is calm.

## What should you do first?

Take a breath, and do not reply in the first few minutes if you feel defensive. Then:

1. **Read the whole message twice.** Underline the specific concerns. Separate facts ("the water bowl was empty") from feelings ("I feel like you did not care").
2. **Check your records.** Look at your visit notes, photos, timestamps, and messages for that day. Your own written record is the most useful thing you have.
3. **Decide what you actually need to know.** If something is unclear, plan to ask a question instead of guessing.

## How do you write the first reply?

Keep it short, warm, and specific. A good first reply often does four things:

- Thanks the client for telling you.
- Says that you take it seriously.
- Restates the concern in your own words so they know you understood.
- Says what you will do next, and by when.

For example: "Thank you for letting me know. I understand that you were hoping to see more detail about Milo's lunch. I am going back through my notes from Tuesday and will reply this evening with what I recorded."

Avoid long explanations in the first message. Avoid blaming the client, the pet, or the weather. You can share context later, once they feel heard.

## What if the complaint is partly your mistake?

Say so plainly. "You are right that I did not send a photo on Tuesday, and I am sorry" is far more convincing than a list of reasons. Then explain what you will change, such as sending an update at the end of every visit. People tend to forgive honest mistakes more easily than excuses.

## What if you think the complaint is unfair?

Stay factual and kind. Share what you recorded, without arguing. For example: "My notes show that I refilled the water at both visits and I have attached the photo from the second one. I understand it may have looked different when you got home. Would it help if I add a bowl photo to every update?" You are not trying to win. You are trying to keep the client's trust and, where reasonable, offer a small change that prevents the same worry.

## Should you reply by text, phone, or email?

Match the client's own style, but keep a written record. If the client calls, you can talk it through and then send a short written summary: "Thanks for talking today. As we discussed, I will send a photo after each visit, starting tomorrow." That summary gives you both a clear record.

## How do you agree on a next step?

End every complaint conversation with one specific action. It might be a change in how you report, a short call, or a decision that the arrangement is not working. Put the decision in writing. If you decide to end the working relationship, do it politely, honor the commitments in your agreement, and thank them for the opportunity to care for their pet.

## How can you prevent complaints in the first place?

Many complaints come from unclear expectations. A written agreement, a clear meet and greet, and consistent visit updates reduce surprises. Clients often feel more comfortable when they can see what happened rather than wonder about it. A short note and one photo after each visit answer many questions before they are asked.
