# Nervous Dogs: What Sitters Should Ask and Record

**A nervous dog needs a sitter who is patient, predictable, and a good note taker. Ask the owner what helps, go slowly, and record what you observe in plain words.** Many dogs relax with time when visits are calm and consistent, and your notes help the owner see the progress.

This is general guidance for independent pet sitters and dog walkers, not veterinary or behavior advice. Follow the owner's instructions, and suggest that the owner talk to their vet or a qualified trainer about any serious behavior concern.

## How do you know a dog is nervous?

The owner will often tell you, but you can also watch for body language: hiding, trembling, backing away, avoiding eye contact, a tucked tail, or a stiff posture. Do not label the dog. Simply describe what you see. Some dogs are shy only with strangers, some only outside, and some in particular situations such as loud noises.

## What should you ask the owner before the first visit?

Good questions include:

- What situations make the dog uncomfortable?
- What are the early signs that the dog is stressed?
- What helps the dog feel calm, such as a favorite spot, toy, or routine?
- How should you approach, and should you wait for the dog to come to you?
- Does the dog wear a harness or collar that must be secure, and how do you attach the leash safely?
- What should you do if the dog will not come out of hiding?
- Are there noises, people, or other animals to be careful about on walks?
- What should you do in an emergency, and who should you contact?

Ask for the routine in as much detail as possible, because predictable visits help nervous dogs.

## How should you handle the first meeting?

Take it slowly. Arrive calm and avoid direct eye contact or sudden movements. Sit quietly for a few minutes if the owner agrees, and let the dog approach in their own time. Speak softly, and offer a treat only if the owner has said it is allowed. A longer meet and greet, or a few short visits before the first full job, can make a big difference.

## What about safety on walks?

Safety is the first priority with a nervous dog, because fear can make a dog try to slip out of a collar or bolt. Make sure the harness or collar fits well, and follow the owner's gear instructions. Choose quiet routes and times where possible. If the dog is uneasy at a particular spot, turn around. A short, calm walk is better than a long, stressful one. If the dog becomes too upset, return home and tell the owner.

## What should you record?

Short, factual notes show progress and help the owner decide what to do next. Include:

- How the dog responded when you arrived.
- Where the dog stayed during the visit.
- Whether the dog ate, drank, and went outside.
- What helped the dog feel more at ease.
- Anything that seemed to upset the dog, and what you did.

Write "Stayed under the table for the first five minutes, then took a treat from my hand and followed me to the door" instead of "was scared at first." Observations without labels are easier for owners to trust.

## How do you update the owner?

Be honest and gentle. Celebrate small steps, such as the first tail wag or the first time the dog came to greet you. If something worrying happens, describe it clearly and ask the owner how they would like to handle it. Avoid blaming the dog or suggesting that the owner has done something wrong.
