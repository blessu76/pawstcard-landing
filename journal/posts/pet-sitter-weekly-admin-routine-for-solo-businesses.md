# A Weekly Admin Routine for Solo Pet Sitters

**Most solo sitters are good with the animals. It is the admin that slips, until invoices go out late and confirmations are forgotten.** A fixed weekly block of about an hour, run the same way every week, keeps the pile from ever forming.

This is general guidance for independent pet sitters and dog walkers. Taxes, pricing, and licensing rules vary by location, so check what applies to your business.

## When should you do your weekly admin?

Pick one day and one time, and keep it. Sunday from 6 to 7 pm works for people who start Monday early; Monday before the first booking works for people who would rather have the weekend free. The exact slot matters less than the repetition: put it on your calendar like a booking, because it is one.

If a week's admin runs over the hour, that is information. It means something in the week was not handled when it happened, and the routine below tells you which step to tighten.

## What goes in the weekly admin block?

Six steps, in this order, in about 60 minutes:

1. **Confirm next week's bookings (10 minutes).** Send confirmations for anything not confirmed, check for double-bookings, and flag gaps you want to fill.
2. **Chase invoices (10 minutes).** Send anything not yet sent, note what is past due, and message the clients who owe you.
3. **Reconcile payments (10 minutes).** Match payments to invoices. If one does not match, write down the open question now while you remember it.
4. **Check the client files (10 minutes).** New clients need a full file: medical info, key location, feeding notes, photo permission. Existing clients may need updates, and you will know because you just checked.
5. **Supplies and gear (5 minutes).** Waste bags, treats, lockbox batteries, a cleaned up kit.
6. **Write next week's plan (15 minutes).** The daily schedule, who has medication, and the one thing to watch for each pet.

Keep a paper or notes-app checklist of the six steps so the routine survives a busy month. If the week was calm, a few steps take five minutes instead of ten, and you have slack for the weeks that run long.

## What should you do daily instead of weekly?

Three daily habits are what keep the weekly block short:

- **Send the visit report after each visit,** while the details are fresh.
- **Jot a one-line daily log** in the evening: what went well, what took longer than planned, anything to ask the client.
- **Record cash payments the same day,** so reconciliation is matching, not remembering.

If you skip these, they pile into the weekly block, and the hour becomes two.

## How do you keep the week from spilling over?

- **Batch the similar work.** All confirmations at once, all invoices at once, not one at a time between visits.
- **Use templates** for the recurring messages: confirmation, invoice reminder, end-of-stay note.
- **Do a monthly hour** on top of the weekly one: taxes and receipts, whether your rates feel right, and the one thing that felt off this month.

## What if a week runs over an hour?

Find the longest step and fix the habit behind it, not the clock. If invoicing takes 30 minutes, you may need to record each visit and payment on the day it happens. If confirmations take 20 minutes, try confirming each booking on the day it is made. The routine is only as good as the small daily habits that feed it, so fix those first and the hour takes care of itself.
