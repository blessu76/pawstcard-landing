# Puppy Drop-In Visits: How Pet Sitters Plan Them

**A good puppy visit is short on surprises and long on routine: confirm the owner's rules in advance, go straight to a potty break, keep play and handling gentle, and write down what happened so the owner can see the pattern.** Puppies often have plenty of energy and little patience for waiting, so a plan for the first five minutes of every visit goes a long way. Puppy care can also be a lot of work, so talk it through with the owner before you say yes.

This is general guidance for independent pet sitters and dog walkers, not veterinary or training advice. Follow the owner's instructions and their vet's guidance, and check any rules that apply where you work.

## Why are puppy visits different?

A puppy is still learning where to go to the bathroom, what to chew, and how to be alone. A visit that works for an adult dog may not work for a puppy. Puppies may need more frequent breaks, closer supervision, and a gentler pace. They also tend to make a mess, so expect cleanup.

Because of this, some sitters set a separate policy for puppies, such as a minimum number of daily visits or a different rate. If you do, include it in your written agreement.

## What should you ask the owner first?

Treat the puppy as a new client with a longer checklist:

- **Vaccinations and vet guidance.** Ask what the owner's vet has said about where the puppy can go and which animals it can meet. Follow that guidance, and do not make your own decisions about it.
- **Feeding.** What food, how much, and when? Is the puppy allowed treats?
- **Potty routine.** Where does the puppy go outside, and what word does the owner use? What does the puppy do just before needing to go?
- **Crate or pen.** Is the puppy crated when alone? How should the crate be set up, and for how long?
- **Chewing and hazards.** What is off limits, and what toys are approved?
- **Training cues.** What words does the owner use, so you stay consistent?
- **Gear.** Does the puppy have a harness, a short leash, or a lead you should use?
- **Emergency details.** The vet's name and phone number, plus a backup contact.

Write all this on a one-page sheet for the home.

## How should you structure the visit?

A simple order works well:

1. **Greet calmly.** Puppies can be excited, so keep your own energy relaxed.
2. **Go outside first.** Take the puppy to the agreed spot right away, using the owner's potty word. If the owner uses praise, praise the puppy when it goes.
3. **Feed and water,** if it is part of the visit.
4. **Play and attention.** A few minutes of gentle interaction. Keep it appropriate for the puppy's age and size, and follow the owner's guidance on exercise.
5. **Clean up.** Handle accidents without scolding, and use the cleaning products the owner has left.
6. **Settle the puppy** in the place the owner described, with water and approved toys.
7. **Check the space** for anything chewed, spilled, or unsafe before you leave.

Keep each step consistent so the puppy learns the pattern.

## What about walks?

Ask the owner what they want. Some owners prefer short outings in a yard or the immediate area, while others follow a plan from their vet. Do not take the puppy to places where it could meet unknown dogs unless the owner has said it is okay. Keep the puppy on a secure leash or harness, and watch for things they might eat from the ground.

## How do you handle accidents and mistakes?

Accidents happen. Stay calm, clean the area with the owner's supplies, and note it in your report. Avoid punishing the puppy. Simply tell the owner what occurred so they can adjust their routine. If the puppy chews something that could be harmful or eats something unusual, contact the owner and the vet they named right away, and follow their instructions. Do not try to treat the puppy yourself.

## What should your update include?

The owner is often keen to know how the day is going. Aim for:

- Time of arrival and leaving
- Potty breaks: when and what happened
- Food and water
- Playtime and how the puppy behaved
- Anything chewed, spilled, or unusual
- One clear photo

Specific details help owners track patterns and share them with their trainer or vet. "Peed twice outside at 12:05 and 12:20, ate all lunch, chewed a slipper, now napping" is much more useful than "all good."

## How do you decide how many puppy clients to take on?

Puppies take time and energy. Think about travel, visit length, and cleanup when you set your schedule. It is fine to decline a request if the schedule would not leave enough time to do the visit properly.
