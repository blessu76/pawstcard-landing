# How to Politely Decline a Pet Sitting Booking

**Declining a booking is not a failure. It is how you protect the quality of the work you already promised to other clients.** A short, kind, clear "no" often leaves a better impression than a reluctant "yes" that turns into a late visit or a rushed note. This article covers when to say no, how to word it, and a few messages you can adapt.

This is general guidance for independent pet sitters and dog walkers. It is not legal or business advice. Check your local rules, your insurance terms, and your own service agreement.

## When is it a good idea to say no?

Everyone's limits are different, but common reasons include:

- **You are already booked.** The dates are full, or taking another visit would push your day past what you can do well. Our article on [planning a day with back-to-back visits](/journal/how-to-plan-a-pet-sitting-day-with-back-to-back-visits/) can help you see where your real limit is.
- **The location is too far.** Travel time counts as work time. If it breaks your route, it breaks your schedule.
- **The animal or the care is outside what you are comfortable with.** Examples include an animal you are not trained to handle, a medical routine you are not comfortable with, or a species you do not normally care for.
- **Your gut says the fit is wrong.** Expectations that do not match your policies, a request to skip a meet and greet, or a client who pushes back on every term in your agreement are signals to take seriously.
- **The request is outside your hours or service.** A 6 a.m. daily visit you do not offer, or a service you do not provide.
- **Safety.** If you do not feel safe at the home or with the animal, you can say no. You do not need to explain more than you want to.

## What makes a polite "no" work?

A good decline has four parts, and it can fit in a few lines:

1. **Thank them.** They chose to ask you.
2. **Say no clearly.** Avoid "maybe" or "I will try" if you mean no. Vague answers make people wait.
3. **Give a short reason, if you want to.** You do not have to. "I am not available for those dates" is enough.
4. **Offer a next step when you can.** A date that works, a referral to a colleague you trust, or a place to look for another sitter.

Reply quickly. A prompt "no" helps the owner find someone else before their trip.

## What can you say? Sample messages

**When you are booked:**
"Thank you so much for thinking of me. I am fully booked for those dates, so I cannot take on Mochi this time. I would love to hear from you early for your next trip, and I am happy to put you on my list for next time."

**When the location is too far:**
"Thank you for reaching out. Your address is outside the area I can cover well, so I do not think I would be the right fit for daily visits. I hope you find someone who can give Max the regular care he deserves."

**When it is outside what you do:**
"Thanks for the details about Bella. I do not currently offer overnight stays, so I would not be able to help with this one. If drop-in visits ever work for you, I would be glad to talk."

**When you have a referral:**
"I am not available for those dates, but I know a sitter, [Name], who covers your neighborhood and does a lovely job. Would you like me to share their contact details?" Only recommend someone you know and trust, and let them know first if you can.

**When the fit does not feel right:**
"Thank you for considering me. After reading through your request, I do not think my service is the right match for what you are looking for, so I will not be able to take this booking. I wish you and your pets the best."

You do not owe a detailed explanation. A short, friendly message is polite and professional.

## What should you avoid?

- **Ghosting.** Not replying leaves the owner stuck.
- **Over-apologizing or over-explaining.** Long reasons invite negotiation.
- **Saying "maybe" to be nice.** You may feel kinder in the moment, but it costs the client time.
- **Criticizing the client or the pet** in your decline. Keep it neutral.
- **Making promises you cannot keep.** Do not say "I will find a way" unless you will.

## How do you decline a client you already work with?

This can feel harder, because there is a relationship to protect. Be warm, specific, and honest about your limits. "I have cut back my weekend visits, so I cannot take Saturday morning walks anymore. I can still do weekday drop-ins." Say it as early as you can, give a date, and if the change is permanent, offer to help them find a replacement. Clients tend to respect a sitter who is clear about their limits.

## How do you make "no" easier next time?

- **Write down your limits** in a short list: service area, hours, services, animals, and lead time.
- **Put those limits on your website or booking form,** so more of the requests you receive already fit.
- **Keep a short template** for each reason, and edit it to sound like you.
- **Keep a short list of trusted colleagues** you can recommend, with their permission.

## A quick checklist before you decline

- Is my reason clear to me, even if I do not share all of it?
- Did I thank them and say no plainly?
- Did I offer a date, a referral, or a next step where it made sense?
- Did I reply quickly enough for them to find someone else?
- Is the message calm, kind, and free of criticism?

A good "no" protects your clients, your schedule, and your reputation, and it leaves the door open for the right booking later.
