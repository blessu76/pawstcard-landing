# Pet Sitter Photo Permission and Client Privacy

**Ask for permission before you photograph, and ask again before you share anything publicly.** Most clients are happy to see their pet, but a photo is also a window into their home, so take only what the visit needs and keep the rest out of frame.

This is general guidance for independent pet sitters and dog walkers, not legal advice. What is acceptable varies by client, by city, and by what your local rules say, so ask each client what they are comfortable with and check what applies where you work.

## What permission should you ask for, and when?

There are two separate permissions, and both deserve a clear yes:

- **Taking photos during the visit** and sending them to the client as updates.
- **Using photos publicly,** on your business page, portfolio, or social accounts.

Ask at the meet-and-greet, out loud, with a real question:

> I send a short update with a couple of photos after each visit. Is that okay, and would you ever be fine with me posting a photo publicly for my page, or should I keep everything between us?

Do not treat silence as consent, and do not bundle the two questions. A client can happily say yes to private updates and no to public posting. Write the answers in the client's notes so the next visit does not re-open the question.

## What should stay out of the frame?

Even with permission, some details should never appear in a photo, because a photo can end up anywhere:

- **House or street numbers** on the door, mailbox, or gate
- **Mail and packages** with the client's name and address
- **Whiteboards, calendars, and notes** that show schedules, routines, or codes
- **Alarm panels, lockboxes, and any visible code**
- **Keys, spare keys, and where they are hidden**
- **Documents, bills, or anything on the counter** that is not the pet

Reframe or crop before you send. If a shot cannot be cleaned up, skip it. Three clean photos of the pet beat one great photo that reveals the home.

## How do you handle a client who says no to photos?

Respect it in the same message, so the boundary is on record:

> Got it, no photos on my end. I will send a written note after each visit instead.

Some clients want a narrower yes: photos of the dog in the yard are fine, but not the house. That is a workable boundary, so ask for scope rather than assuming all or nothing. If a client changes their mind mid-job, just update your notes. You do not need to make a big deal of it either way.

## How do you store and share photos safely?

- **Keep one folder or album per pet,** named after the pet, so updates never cross clients.
- **Do not post location tags or street details** in captions. "Sitting with a great pup in the city today" is fine. "Day off with Biscuit on Maple Street" is not.
- **Do not include other people's pets** in a photo you send to one client, and avoid screenshots of client chats where other pets or homes appear.
- **Delete on request.** If a client asks you to remove a photo, do it the same day and confirm it is done.

The habit that makes all of this easy: treat every photo as if it could be forwarded to the client's whole family, because it might be.

## What if a photo accidentally shows something private?

If you notice before sending, crop it and move on. If it is already sent, say so quickly, delete the photo, and name the fix:

> You are right, the mailbox number is in the second photo. I have deleted it from my side, and I will crop carefully on the rest. Sorry about that.

A fast, honest fix costs you almost nothing. A covered-up mistake costs more, because the client is now watching every photo.
