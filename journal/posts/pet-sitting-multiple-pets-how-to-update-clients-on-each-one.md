# Sitting Multiple Pets: How to Update Clients on Each One

**In a multi-pet home, treat each animal as its own small job: learn each pet's routine, check each one at every visit, and give the client one clear line per pet.** It is easy to write "all good!" for a household of three and miss that one cat skipped her dinner. A short, repeatable structure keeps every pet seen and gives clients the specific detail they are looking for.

This is general guidance for independent sitters and dog walkers. Every household is different, so follow the client's instructions, and call the client or vet if something worries you.

## Why are multi-pet households harder?

There are more routines, more tasks, and more places for a detail to slip. Different pets may eat different food, take different medications, or need to be kept apart at meals. One may be shy while another demands all the attention. The visit may be a little shorter per pet, but the checklist is longer.

The risk is that the loudest or friendliest pet becomes the one you remember, while the quiet one disappears from your notes.

## What should you learn at the meet-and-greet?

Ask about the group as well as each individual:

1. **Each pet's feeding plan.** Who eats what, how much, and where
2. **Who gets along, and who does not.** Any pets that must be separated, and for how long
3. **Medications by pet.** Written down separately, with the right names attached
4. **Walks.** Together or one at a time, and who pulls, who is reactive, who is slow
5. **Hiding spots and escape habits.** Especially for cats, who may be hard to find
6. **Where each pet sleeps** and any places they should not go
7. **Signs of stress** for each one, as the client sees them

If possible, meet all the pets at the consultation. Notice how they behave around each other and around you.

## How do you run a visit with several pets?

A simple order keeps it from feeling chaotic:

1. **Count heads first.** Confirm every pet is present and looks normal before you do anything else.
2. **Feed separately if needed,** and watch each pet finish. A pet that eats quickly may help itself to another's bowl.
3. **Do medications one pet at a time,** with the label in your hand.
4. **Give each pet some one-on-one time,** even a minute or two.
5. **Walk in the order the client suggests.** A group walk is not always the right choice.
6. **Do a final count** before you leave.

Counting at both ends of the visit is a small habit that catches missing or hidden pets.

## How should you write the update?

Use one line per pet, with the same details each time. It makes the note easy to scan:

> **Milo:** ate all of his dinner, one long walk, back to his usual spot on the couch.
> **Pepper:** ate about half and picked at the rest. Drank water. Playful afterward.
> **Biscuit (cat):** came out from under the bed for treats, ate everything, litter box scooped.

Then add the shared part, such as the time you arrived and left. If any pet had a problem, put that first, as you would in a single-pet update.

Some tips:

- **Use names, not "the dogs."** Clients notice when you are specific.
- **Keep the same order each day,** so changes are easy to see.
- **Include at least one specific detail per pet.** Even "ignored his toy" says you were paying attention.
- **Do not skip the quiet one.** A shy pet still deserves a line.

## What if the pets do not get along?

- **Follow the client's separation plan** exactly, including doors and gates
- **Do not try to settle disputes** by putting pets together unless the client has said it is fine
- **Report any tension** you saw, such as growling or stiff body language, in plain terms
- **Write down what happened and when,** and tell the client the same day

If you ever feel a situation is unsafe, step back, separate the pets as the client taught you, and contact the client.

## How should you handle photos?

Aim for at least one photo per pet across the stay, but not necessarily in every update. A single group shot is nice, and one clear photo of each pet shows that you saw them individually. Do not chase every pet for a picture if it will stress them.
