# How Pet Sitters Can Ask for Reviews and Referrals Without Awkwardness

**Ask for a review or a referral right after a stay that went well, once, in a short message that makes it easy to say yes and easy to say no.** Independent sitters often grow through word of mouth, but many feel shy about asking. The fix is a simple routine: pick the right moment, keep the request small, and thank people either way.

This is general guidance for independent sitters and dog walkers. If you work through a platform or have a business license, check the platform's rules and any local rules about reviews and incentives that apply to you.

## Why do reviews and referrals matter?

For a solo sitter, new clients mostly arrive because someone trusted them. A review gives a stranger a reason to feel comfortable, and a referral gives them a personal introduction. Neither works without a happy client who thinks to mention you, and clients are busy. A gentle prompt helps them remember.

## When is the best time to ask?

Timing does most of the work:

- **Right after the stay,** while the client is relaxed and the pet is happy to be home
- **After a nice moment,** such as a client thanking you or sharing a fond reply
- **After a few days,** if you prefer to let them settle in first

Avoid asking:

- In the middle of a stay
- After a problem that has not been resolved
- Right after a payment reminder
- Repeatedly. One request and, at most, one gentle follow-up is plenty.

## How do you ask for a review?

Make it a small ask and make the path clear. Say where you would like the review, and link directly if you can.

> Hi Sam, thank you for trusting me with Juno this week. It was a pleasure. If you have a minute, a short review would help other pet parents find me. Here is the link: [link]. No pressure at all, and I'm happy to take care of her again anytime.

A few principles:

- **Be specific about what you are asking for.** One link, one request.
- **Say thanks first.** The message is a thank-you with a small ask attached.
- **Make it optional,** and mean it.
- **Do not write the review for the client.** You can suggest what they might mention, such as the updates or communication, but leave the words to them.
- **Do not offer money or gifts in return for a review** unless you have checked that the platform and local rules allow it. Reviews that are paid for may be disallowed on some platforms.

## How do you ask for a referral?

A referral request works best when it is personal and low-stakes:

> If you know anyone nearby with a dog or cat who might need a sitter, I'd be glad to meet them. Feel free to share my number, or I can send you a short message to forward.

Some sitters offer a small thank-you for referrals, such as a discount on the next stay. If you do, keep it simple, say it is optional, and make sure it is allowed under any rules that apply to you.

Useful ideas:

- **Make it easy to forward.** A short intro message the client can paste saves them effort.
- **Ask neighbors and friends of clients,** who often have similar needs.
- **Thank people who refer you,** even if the referral does not book.

## What should you do with feedback?

- **Thank the client** for every review, short or long
- **Reply politely to criticism.** Thank them, acknowledge their experience, and say what you will do differently. Do not argue in public.
- **Use what you learn.** If three clients mention the same thing, treat it as a signal.
- **Ask permission before quoting a review** on your own site or materials, if it is not already public

## What if the client is not happy?

Handle the problem privately first. If a stay did not go as the client hoped, they need a reply and maybe a fix, not a review request. Listen, respond calmly, and see whether you can make it right. Asking for a review in that moment can come across as tone-deaf.

## What makes people more likely to say yes?

Clients tend to review sitters who gave them good reasons: clear updates, honest communication, and no surprises. The work you do during the stay shapes the answer you get afterward. A client who got a thoughtful update every day is more likely to remember that when you ask.
