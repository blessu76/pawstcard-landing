# After a Pet Sitting Stay: Follow-Up and Repeat Bookings

**The day a pet comes home is one of the best moments to earn a repeat booking, because the client has just seen how the stay went.** Send the end-of-stay summary the same day the pet walks back in, then send a short check-in within a day or two.

This is general guidance for independent pet sitters and dog walkers. Follow any agreement you have with the client and any review rules that apply to your business.

## When should you send the end-of-stay summary?

The same day the pet comes home, or first thing the next morning. Do not wait three days. While the client is home, they are asking relatives how the pet did, comparing notes with the neighbor, and quietly figuring out who gets the next trip. A sitter who sends a complete, warm summary before that conversation ends is easy to remember.

If the pet comes home at 8 pm, a summary that goes out at 9 pm beats one that goes out Tuesday.

## What should the follow-up message include?

One message, short, with four parts:

1. **A confirmation.** "Hope the flight back was smooth."
2. **A warm detail from the stay.** "Koda did great all week. He settled into the routine by day two and was chasing the feather toy happily by day four."
3. **A pointer to the full summary.** "The full end-of-stay note is at the link below, with the daily notes and the home checklist."
4. **An open question.** "Did everything go smoothly when you got home?"

> Hope you made it home without a delay! Luna settled right back into her routine. The full end-of-stay summary, with daily notes and the house walkthrough, is at the link below. Did everything go smoothly on your end?

## How do you ask for a review without being pushy?

One clear ask, tied to the follow-up, and then stop.

> If the stay went well and you have two minutes, a short review would mean a lot to my small business. The link is here. No pressure at all.

Do not ask again in the same thread, and do not stack a discount offer on top of the ask in the same message unless you genuinely mean to give one. A single gentle nudge a week later is fine if they said they would write one. If they simply do not, let it go. The follow-up itself does most of the retention work.

## How do you position yourself for the next booking?

Three small moves:

- **Offer dates, casually.** "I am happy to hold dates for your next trip if you give me a week's notice." Many clients are already thinking about the next trip.
- **Note the pattern in your client file.** School breaks, holidays, an annual conference. When the pattern returns, a one-line reminder is welcome, not spam: "Thinking ahead, the school break dates are coming up. Want me to hold them?"
- **Keep the door open in the summary itself.** A last line like "See you next summer" tells the client the relationship continues, not that the invoice closed it.

## What if the stay had a problem?

Sequence matters: the follow-up goes first, and the review ask does not.

> I want to start with the limp on Wednesday. Here is what happened, what we did, and the note from your vet's office. I have written the full details up in the summary. I am sorry it added a wrinkle, and I would like to talk about how I would handle it differently if it happens again.

Apologize once, briefly, for the impact. State the facts and the follow-through. Offer a make-up for the affected days if that is your policy. Then wait. Clients often trust a sitter more after seeing a problem handled honestly and calmly.
