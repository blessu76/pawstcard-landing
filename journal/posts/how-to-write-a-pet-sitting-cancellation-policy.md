# How to Write a Pet Sitting Cancellation Policy Clients Respect

**A good cancellation policy answers four questions in plain words: how much notice a client should give, what happens if they give less, how date changes are handled, and what you do if you need to cancel.** The goal is not to punish anyone. It is to protect time that you set aside for a pet and could have offered to someone else. Written clearly and shared early, a policy rarely feels harsh, because the client has already agreed to it.

This is general guidance for independent sitters and dog walkers, not legal advice. Rules about fees and contracts vary by location, so check what applies to your business.

## Why does a pet sitter need a cancellation policy?

As a solo sitter, your calendar is your inventory. When a client cancels the day before a holiday trip, the slots you held cannot always be filled again. Without a stated policy, you have two bad choices: absorb the loss quietly, or have an awkward conversation after the fact. A policy lets you skip that conversation.

It also helps clients. People like knowing the rules before they book, especially when plans are uncertain.

## What should the policy cover?

Keep it to one page or less. These are the parts most sitters include:

1. **Notice period.** How far ahead a client should cancel to avoid a fee. Some sitters use a shorter window for ordinary weeks and a longer one for busy dates such as holidays.
2. **What happens inside that window.** A fee, a partial fee, or none. State the amount or the method, not just "a fee may apply."
3. **Late changes.** Shortening a stay, moving a start date, or adding visits. Say what you can accommodate and what depends on your calendar.
4. **Same-day cancellations.** For example, a client who is home early or a walk that is no longer needed.
5. **Deposits.** If you take one for peak dates, say whether and when it is refundable.
6. **Your own cancellations.** What you will do if you get sick or have an emergency, such as tell the client right away and, if you can, suggest another sitter you trust.
7. **Weather and safety.** If travel is unsafe, how you will communicate and whether visits can be rescheduled.

## How do you word it so it sounds fair?

Tone matters as much as terms. Compare these two:

> Cancellations within 48 hours are non-refundable.

> If you need to cancel with less than 48 hours' notice, I charge half of the booked visits, because I will have held those times for your pet. Earlier than that, there is no charge.

The second says the same thing but explains the reason. (The 48 hours and the half charge are only examples; set terms that fit your business and your area.) Add one sentence of context and most clients nod and move on.

A few wording tips:

- **Lead with the easy case.** "Cancel with plenty of notice and there is no charge."
- **Explain the reason once,** without apology.
- **Use numbers and dates,** not "reasonable notice."
- **Avoid threatening language.** You are describing how you work, not warning anyone.

## When should you make an exception?

Policies are tools, not walls. Some sitters waive a fee when:

- The client is a long-time regular and the change is genuinely unavoidable
- A pet's health or a family emergency is involved
- You were able to refill the slot with another booking

If you make an exception, say it is an exception: "I'm happy to waive the fee this time." That keeps the policy intact for the next client and keeps the goodwill. Decide in advance what you are comfortable waiving so you are not improvising under pressure.

## How and when should you share it?

- **Before the first booking is confirmed,** ideally in the same message or form as your rates
- **At the meet-and-greet,** briefly, so it is spoken as well as written
- **Again before peak dates,** with a short reminder
- **In writing,** so the client can confirm it by reply

A one-line acceptance such as "Reply 'agreed' to confirm" works for many sitters.

## How should you respond when a client cancels?

Stay calm and kind. A short reply is usually enough:

> Thanks for letting me know. I've removed the visits from my calendar. As we discussed, the late-notice charge applies to the Friday visits, and I'll include it on your invoice. I hope the trip still goes well, and I'm happy to rebook anytime.

Then update your calendar and, if the dates were in demand, tell anyone on your waiting list.
