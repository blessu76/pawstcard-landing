# Can't Find the Cat? What to Do on a Visit

**If you cannot find the cat when you arrive, do not panic and do not chase. Check the safe, likely places first, make sure no door or window is the reason, and call the owner if you have not found the cat within a few minutes.** Cats are good at disappearing inside a home, and often the cat is simply hiding. But a missing cat is also one of the situations where a calm, written process matters most.

This is general guidance for independent pet sitters. It is not veterinary, legal, or insurance advice. Follow the emergency and lost-pet instructions the owner gave you, and your own insurance terms.

## Why do cats hide during a sitter's visit?

A new person, a change in routine, a noise, or simply a nap in an odd place can be enough. Some cats hide when the owner is away, especially with a visitor they do not know well yet. Hiding alone does not tell you what is going on, but it is worth writing down, because the owner knows whether it is normal for this cat.

## What should you do in the first few minutes?

Stay quiet and slow. A fast, loud search can push a nervous cat deeper into hiding.

1. **Check that the home is secure first.** Before anything else, look at every outside door, window, cat flap, and balcony. If one is open, ajar, or has a loose screen, treat the situation as more urgent and move to the "call the owner" step below.
2. **Look in the places the owner told you about.** This is why the meet and greet matters. Ask beforehand: "Where does she like to hide? Where does she nap?" Typical spots include under or inside beds and couches, closets, behind curtains, inside boxes, behind the washing machine, and on top of shelves.
3. **Use the cat's routines.** Shake the treat bag, open a can, or fill the food bowl quietly. A cat that is hiding but not distressed will often come out for its usual meal.
4. **Look up and look small.** Cats can fit into spaces that seem too narrow, and many sit high. Check shelves, the tops of cabinets, and the back of closets with a flashlight (your phone light works).
5. **Check gaps and appliances you can safely check.** Look behind and under furniture, in laundry baskets, in open drawers, and in the dryer or cabinets that might have been left open. Never run an appliance until you are sure no one is inside it.

## When should you call the owner?

Decide this ahead of time and write it down. A reasonable default is: if you cannot find the cat after about ten minutes of calm searching, or sooner if a door or window might have been open, call the owner. Many owners would rather get an early call than a late one, and they often know a hiding place you would never guess. If the owner is unreachable, use the emergency contact named in your service agreement.

If a cat may have gotten outside, follow the lost-pet steps you agreed on before the stay. Our article on [what to do if a pet gets out during a visit](/journal/what-to-do-if-a-pet-gets-out-during-a-sitting-visit/) walks through that situation in more detail.

## What if the cat is found but behaving strangely?

If you find the cat but it is hiding in an unusual place, hissing, will not eat, or seems very quiet, do not guess at a cause. Write down what you saw, how long it lasted, and what you did, and share it with the owner. If the cat shows signs that could be an emergency, such as trouble breathing, repeated vomiting, or collapse, follow the emergency plan and call the vet or clinic named in it. For more on wording, see [how to write about a pet's health changes without diagnosing](/journal/how-to-write-about-a-pets-health-changes-without-diagnosing/).

## What should you write in the visit note?

Even when everything ends well, write it down. A short, factual note might read:

> "Mochi was not in her usual spot when I arrived at 4:10 p.m. I checked the doors and windows (all closed and locked) and looked under the bed and in the hall closet. I rattled the treat bag and she came out from behind the sofa after about four minutes. She ate her dinner and used the litter box. She seemed fine. I wanted you to know in case this is unusual for her."

A good note includes:

- **The time you arrived and when you found the cat.**
- **What you checked first**, especially that doors and windows were secure.
- **What you did to find her**, in plain words.
- **How she was when found**, described, not diagnosed.
- **Whether you contacted the owner**, and when.

## How can you reduce the chance of a long search?

- **At the meet and greet, ask where the cat hides** and how long it normally takes to come out.
- **Learn the layout.** Note the closets, cabinets, and gaps the owner points out.
- **Agree on the call threshold.** Write down how many minutes you will search before phoning, and who to call.
- **Photograph the cat.** Ask the owner for a clear, recent photo and a description of any markings, in case you ever need to share it.
- **Close doors behind you** and confirm the same at every visit so no one is relying on memory.

## A short checklist for a missing-cat moment

- Did I check that every door, window, and cat flap is secure?
- Did I look in the spots the owner told me about, calmly and quietly?
- Did I note the time I started looking?
- Did I call the owner by the time we agreed on, or sooner if a door was open?
- Did I write down what I saw and what I did?

If you can answer yes to each, you did what a careful sitter can do, and you gave the owner the facts they need.
